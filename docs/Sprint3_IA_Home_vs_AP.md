## Purpose of This Document

Define and compare the information architecture of the Home page and the AP page so that each page solves a different user problem with different storytelling. This document must be approved before any implementation changes.

## Home Page

### Primary Goal

Introduce AP Future as an education company and give a high-level overview of each program. The page builds trust and guides visitors toward a consultation.

### Target Audience

First-time visitors — Grade 8–12 students and parents who are beginning to explore university preparation options and may not yet know which program they need.

### User Questions Answered

1. Who are we?
2. What services do we provide?
3. Why should families trust AP Future?

### Section Order

| # | Section | Why It Exists |
|---|---------|---------------|
| 1 | **Hero** | Company tagline and primary CTA. Sets the tone — confident, professional, education-first. Gives visitors an immediate sense of what AP Future offers and a path to act. |
| 2 | **Who We Are** | A centered narrative paragraph that answers "Who are we?" in plain language. This is intentionally not a card grid — it reads as a company introduction, not a feature list. Differentiates the Home page from program pages that lead with cards. |
| 3 | **What We Do** | Four programs presented as alternating rows with dividers, each with an icon, description, and individual "Explore" link. This layout avoids the uniform 4-column card grid used on program pages. Each program gets equal weight with a direct link to its dedicated page. |
| 4 | **Why Families Trust Us** | Four trust pillars (Expert Instructors, Proven Approach, Global Perspective, Personalized Guidance). Answers "Why should families trust AP Future?" without fabricated statistics or testimonials, per the About page rules. |
| 5 | **CTA** | Centered call to action for consultation booking. The final step — visitors who have read the company intro and services can now take action. |

### Why This Structure

The Home page tells a company story: identity → services → trust → action. It uses narrative text and alternating rows instead of card grids to create a distinct visual rhythm from program pages. The page stays high-level — it does not list individual courses, exam details, or planning steps. Those belong on the program pages.

## AP Page

### Primary Goal

Help parents understand AP and decide whether it is the right academic pathway for their child. The page educates, provides a course catalog, and guides course selection.

### Target Audience

Parents of Grade 8–12 students who are evaluating AP as an option. They may not fully understand what AP is, why it matters, or how to choose the right courses. They need education before they need a catalog.

### User Questions Answered

1. Why should students take AP?
2. Which AP courses fit different university goals?
3. How should students choose courses?
4. Why learn AP with AP Future?

### Section Order

| # | Section | Why It Exists |
|---|---------|---------------|
| 1 | **Hero** | AP-specific headline focused on the decision parents face. Eyebrow "Advanced Placement", title speaks to choosing the right courses. Two CTAs: Book a Consultation (primary) and "Why AP?" (secondary, scrolls to the Why AP section). This is not a company tagline — it is a decision prompt. |
| 2 | **Why AP Matters** | Educational section that answers "Why should students take AP?" Explains the concrete benefits of AP: university credit, demonstrated academic rigor, competitive advantage in admissions, and global recognition. This section uses a narrative intro followed by 3–4 key benefits in a layout that is NOT a card grid (e.g., numbered list with descriptions, or an editorial two-column layout). This is the section that most differentiates the AP page from a catalog — it provides context before showing courses. |
| 3 | **Course Catalog** | All 13 AP courses grouped into Mathematics, Physics, and Social Sciences. Each course is a CourseCard with title, category, description, recommended grade, difficulty, and duration. This is the reference section — parents can see the full offering. Section header explains that courses follow the College Board curriculum. This section answers "Which AP courses fit different university goals?" by organizing courses by discipline. |
| 4 | **How to Choose** | Recommended course combinations (pathways) aligned with common university majors: Engineering, Computer Science, Business, Social Sciences. Each pathway lists the recommended courses and why. This section answers "How should students choose courses?" It is decision support, not just a list. Uses a different layout from the Course Catalog — e.g., numbered or stepped layout, not a 4-column card grid. |
| 5 | **Why AP Future** | What makes learning AP with AP Future different from self-study or school-only instruction. Focuses on teaching approach: experienced instructors, structured curriculum, personalized support, and exam preparation. This section answers "Why learn AP with AP Future?" It is intentionally distinct from the Home page's "Why Families Trust Us" — the Home page trust pillars are company-level, while this section is AP-specific and focused on instructional quality. Layout differs: e.g., a two-column comparison or a single narrative with inline highlights, not a 4-column card grid. |
| 6 | **FAQ** | Six common questions parents and students ask about AP: how many courses to take, when to start, whether AP is only for US universities, how AP differs from regular courses, what to do if struggling, and exam registration. Provides reassurance and practical guidance. |
| 7 | **CTA** | AP-specific call to action: "Ready to Start Your AP Journey?" with Book a Consultation (primary) and Explore SAT (secondary). The final step for parents who have decided AP is right for their child. |

### Why This Structure

The AP page tells a decision story: understand → browse → choose → trust → act. It leads with education (Why AP Matters) before showing the catalog, because parents need to understand the value of AP before evaluating individual courses. The course catalog is the core reference section, but it is not the first thing visitors see. The "How to Choose" section provides decision support that a pure catalog cannot. "Why AP Future" is AP-specific instructional quality, not a repeat of the company-level trust pillars on the Home page.

## How the Pages Differ

| Dimension | Home Page | AP Page |
|-----------|-----------|---------|
| **Opening section after Hero** | Who We Are — narrative company intro | Why AP Matters — educational benefits of AP |
| **Main content type** | Service overview (alternating rows) | Course catalog (grouped card grid) |
| **Card usage** | Minimal — 4 trust pillars only | Heavy — 13 course cards + pathway items |
| **Decision support** | None — each service links to its own page | Pathways section with recommended combinations |
| **Trust section** | Company-level: Expert Instructors, Proven Approach, Global Perspective, Personalized Guidance | AP-specific: teaching approach, curriculum structure, exam preparation, instructor expertise |
| **FAQ** | None | Yes — 6 AP-specific questions |
| **Storytelling rhythm** | Identity → services → trust → action | Educate → catalog → guide → reassure → act |
| **Visual rhythm** | Narrative text + alternating rows + pillar grid | Benefits layout + card grid + stepped layout + comparison + accordion |

## Component Usage Plan

### Home Page (unchanged from Sprint 3)

- Hero (split)
- CTA (centered)
- PageContainer (wide)
- No CourseCard, no FeatureGrid, no FAQAccordion

### AP Page (proposed)

- Hero (split) — AP-specific copy
- CourseCard x13 — course catalog (existing component, unchanged)
- FeatureGrid — used only for "How to Choose" pathways section (existing component, unchanged)
- FAQAccordion — FAQ section (existing component, unchanged)
- CTA (centered) — AP-specific copy
- PageContainer — section wrapper (existing component, unchanged)
- "Why AP Matters" and "Why AP Future" sections use custom inline layouts (not existing card components) to create visual distinction from the Home page

No new reusable components are created. No existing components are modified. Custom layouts are page-specific markup within the AP page file, not new components.
